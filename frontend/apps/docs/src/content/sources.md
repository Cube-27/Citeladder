**Tracking → Sources** helps you move from "our visibility changed" to the pages used in the measured answers. It has five tabs: **Domains**, **URLs**, **Read, not cited**, **Query fan-outs** and **Ads**. The run, engine, prompts, market and topic filters above the tabs apply to every tab.

## Start broad, then inspect a page

1. Select the audit, engine and topic you want to investigate.
2. On **Domains**, review recurring domains across that selection, with their usage over time, their types and the **Top movers** that rose or fell.
3. Open a domain to inspect its specific URLs and connected prompts, or switch to **URLs** for every cited page. Set **Standing** to **Gaps** to see pages that list competitors but not you.
4. Open an original answer to see the citation in context.

Search, sort and export work on each table. A frequently used domain can be worth investigating. Frequency alone does not tell you whether a particular page mentions your brand or whether you can influence it.

CiteLadder also groups cited competitor and publisher content pages into content angles. Each angle your own pages are not cited for becomes an Action; see [Content optimization](/content-optimization/).

## Retrieval and citation are different

A page can be retrieved without being cited. CiteLadder's citation rate uses the responses in which that source was retrieved as its denominator.

Do not divide a source's citations by all responses in the selection and label the result as the same citation rate.

**Read, not cited** lists your own pages that engines read while answering, with how many answers read them, cited them and did not cite them. Those are the pages closest to a citation. Engines that do not expose the pages they read are not counted.

## Read brand lists carefully

A brand listed beside a cited URL co-occurred in answers that cited the URL. That does not prove the publisher's page mentions the brand, recommends it or supplied the claim.

For example, an answer can cite a general industry report and independently mention a software vendor. Inspecting the report is a separate step.

## Read query fanouts

**Query fan-outs** lists the searches engines reported running while they answered, exactly as the engine returned them. For each search it shows the surface, its **Occurrences**, its **Share** of every search in the selection, its **Change** in share since the previous comparable run, and **Brands matched**, the tracked brands the search names. Switch between **Top**, **Rising** and **Falling** to see the largest searches or the ones that moved most, and group them by prompt or topic.

Change appears only when your selection holds one run and an earlier comparable run exists. Brands read **Not counted** for runs collected before brand matching on searches existed. The answer filters (prompt, outcome, competitor, domain or URL) narrow the searches too.

Some engines search without reporting what they searched for, and absence of captured fanout does not prove that an engine did no research.

## Ads in ChatGPT answers

**Ads** shows the paid ads that appeared in ChatGPT Search answers in the selected runs. Ads are reported on their own: they never count as citations or sources and never change mention rate, share of voice or scores.

- **Ad presence** is the share of ChatGPT Search answers that showed at least one ad, written as "N of M ChatGPT Search answers".
- **Your ad share** is your share of every ad appearance, with your best ad position. When none of your ads was seen it reads "Not advertising", not 0%.
- **Advertisers seen** counts the distinct advertisers.
- **Advertisers** lists who advertised, whether each is you, a competitor or another business, how often and on how many prompts, and when they were first and last seen.
- **Prompts that surface ads** lists the prompts whose answers showed ads, the top advertiser, and how often the answer still mentioned you when a competitor advertised. These are counts, not a cause.
- **Ad creatives** lists each ad as text with the page it leads to. Tracking parameters are removed and ad images are not shown.

Only ChatGPT Search shows ads. Other surfaces read **Not applicable**, never zero. ChatGPT Search answers collected before ads were read show ads as unavailable. Ads are those seen in ChatGPT sessions collected for your project's market: what your buyers see depends on their plan, account and country.

Each ChatGPT Search answer in a run also lists its ads under the answer, separate from its citations.

## Work on an earned Action

CiteLadder reads pages that AI answers cite. When a cited page lists one of your competitors and does not list you, it opens an earned Action for that page. Read the saved reading and its date before planning outreach.

Use the Agent to prepare a reviewable brief. Your team decides whether and how to contact a publisher; CiteLadder does not send outreach or secure placement automatically.

## Verify the right thing

An earned Action is measured on the tracked prompts whose answers cited the page: after you declare it implemented, later visibility runs show whether those prompts moved. That reading does not prove the listing caused the change.

Use [Actions and measurement](/agent/actions/) to understand how implementation and later checks are connected.
