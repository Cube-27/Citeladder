You will need access to a CiteLadder workspace, your website address and a clear idea of the market you serve. Available runs and features depend on your workspace’s access and allowances.

## 1. Review your company facts

Create a project from your website. Review the business category, buyer type, market, owned domains and suggested competitors before confirming.

Suggestions are a starting point. Remove companies that do not compete for your actual buyers and correct ambiguous names or domains. Review inferred positioning, audience and offerings in your project as well; discovered text is not automatically a confirmed company fact.

**You are ready to continue when:** the project describes your business and intended audience accurately.

## 2. Review your prompts

Open **Prompts** after the initial portfolio finishes generating. Read the questions as if you were a potential customer. Keep a focused set that covers the decisions buyers make, and distinguish general discovery questions from questions that name your brand.

For a fictional scheduling product, a useful discovery question might be “Which scheduling tools work well for a small consultancy?” A question such as “How does Example Scheduler handle reminders?” answers a different, brand-specific question.

See [Prompts and topics](/prompts/) before expanding the portfolio.

## 3. Collect the baseline you need

For AI search, start an audit with the prompts, engines and repetitions you intend to measure. Review its estimate and confirm the run. Check **Runs** for completion and failures.

For website evidence, open **Site Health** and choose **Run new crawl**. Creating a project does not automatically start its first crawl.

For search performance, connect a supported provider and map the correct property to this project. Wait for imported coverage before interpreting an empty chart.

> An estimate is a preview. It does not start a run, and it is not the final measured cost.

## 4. Inspect one result

Open an AI answer, an affected website page or a search query behind a summary. Check the observation date and scope. If a result is partial or unavailable, read the reason before treating it as a problem with your brand.

The goal is one finding you can explain with evidence, rather than a long list of numbers.

## 5. Take it into the Agent

Open a relevant **Action** and select **Work on this**, or use **Ask agent** on a supported evidence screen. Check the attached references in the composer, then describe the outcome you need.

Try: “Explain this finding, show the evidence behind it, and propose a small page edit for review.”

Review the result before using it. Once your team actually makes the change, follow [implementation and measurement](/agent/actions/#declare-implementation).

## If setup does not finish

Keep the existing project and inspect its displayed status before creating another. Initial prompt generation can finish after the project is created. A provider failure or unavailable dataset needs its own recovery; repeatedly opening the page does not rerun it.

For access and project selection, see [Workspaces and access](/workspaces/).
