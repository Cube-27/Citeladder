## Is SEO being replaced by AI?

**AI is changing search discovery and automating parts of SEO work, but that does not establish that SEO as a whole is being replaced.** Separate three questions: whether users still search, whether search sends clicks to websites, and whether individual SEO tasks can be automated. Each needs different evidence and a different business response.

A search engine can remain heavily used while sending fewer visits to a particular publisher. An AI assistant can become a popular research tool while users still open product pages to check prices, evidence or availability.

For a website owner, the useful question is how customers discover information and what makes visiting your website worthwhile.

## Three different meanings of "AI replacing SEO"

| Claim                                | Evidence needed                                       | What it would mean for a website                   |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| AI replaces traditional search usage | Comparable user behaviour over time                   | Discovery strategy needs to follow the audience    |
| AI answers reduce outbound clicks    | Click behaviour for defined searches and surfaces     | Traffic forecasts and page value need reassessment |
| AI replaces SEO tasks or jobs        | Task-level quality, costs and organisational evidence | Roles and workflows may change                     |

A study about one row cannot settle the others. A citation-error benchmark does not measure search adoption, and a traffic decline does not by itself prove that an entire profession is obsolete.

## What the click data shows

Pew Research Center's 2025 analysis of browsing from 900 US adults reported traditional-result clicks on 8% of visits with an AI summary versus 15% without one. Search-result content was collected after the March browsing period, which limits interpretation.[\[1\]](#source-1)

Our recalculation makes the difference easier to read:

| Expression of the same published comparison    |                                   Value |
| ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------: |
| Absolute difference                            |                     7 percentage points |
| Relative difference, using 15% as the baseline |                             46.7% lower |
| Illustrated per 1,000 comparable visits        | 80 versus 150 traditional-result clicks |

The final row is a rescaling of published rates, not 1,000 new observations. This is not proof that every website lost 46.7% of traffic, nor a causal estimate from randomly assigning summaries.

The distinction matters when someone turns an observational headline into a revenue forecast.

### What newer research adds

An August 2026 preprint reports a preregistered field experiment involving 1,100 participants and finds that removing AI search features increased publisher click-through in its setting.[\[2\]](#source-2) This is a different research design from an observational panel, but it still does not predict the effect for every query, industry or future interface.

A separate July 2026 journal article, based on a 266-person survey, found patterns more consistent with complementary use of search and generative AI than immediate replacement.[\[3\]](#source-3) Its non-random, predominantly younger sample and self-reported preferences limit generalisation.

Read these findings together as evidence of changing behaviour, not as a single universal forecast.

## Why zero-click discovery changes the value of a page

Some questions can be satisfied in the answer itself. If your page only restates a short public definition, readers may have less reason to open it after receiving a usable summary.

Other tasks still benefit from a destination:

- Applying a calculation to personal inputs.
- Inspecting a complete dataset or methodology.
- Comparing detailed constraints.
- Using a product, downloading a template or completing a transaction.
- Checking an authoritative record.
- Reading a first-hand account that adds context beyond the summary.

This is an editorial opportunity, not a guarantee of traffic. Ask what the reader can do on your page that the short answer cannot finish for them.

## Which SEO work is likely to change?

AI tools can assist with drafting, grouping questions, summarising crawl findings and preparing analysis. That changes how people do the work; it does not remove the need to check whether the output is accurate and appropriate.

A useful division of responsibility is:

| Work                | Useful AI assistance                        | Human responsibility                                         |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Topic planning      | Organise candidate questions                | Confirm audience need and distinct intent                    |
| Content drafting    | Produce an initial structure or explanation | Supply expertise, verify facts and remove unsupported claims |
| Technical diagnosis | Summarise evidence and suggest hypotheses   | Validate the cause and assess deployment risk                |
| Research            | Find candidate sources                      | Check provenance, methodology and interpretation             |
| Reporting           | Calculate and format defined metrics        | Choose meaningful outcomes and explain uncertainty           |

This is a recommended workflow, not a forecast of headcount reductions.

## What remains important when AI mediates discovery?

### Reliable information

A company still needs accurate product details, clear policies, useful documentation and evidence for its claims. Automating content production without maintaining those facts can multiply contradictions.

### Technical accessibility

Search and retrieval systems need a usable path to public information. Broken pages and unclear site structure remain practical problems even when the final interface is conversational.

### Distinctive value

Original examples, first-hand knowledge, transparent research and useful tools give people reasons to use a page. Rewriting the same summary in a new tone offers little differentiation.

### Measurement discipline

Clicks, mentions, citations and conversions answer different questions. A business should know which changed before deciding that its entire search strategy failed.

## What should website owners do now?

### 1. Segment the exposure instead of panicking over total traffic

Separate informational pages from product, support, local and transactional journeys. Examine which queries and landing pages changed, along with seasonality, technical changes and competition.

Do not assign every decline to AI because AI adoption is increasing.

### 2. Upgrade the pages most vulnerable to summary-only answers

Add something the reader can use: a decision tree, transparent calculation, downloadable research inputs, a worked implementation or a clear comparison of trade-offs.

Do not pad the page to make it harder to summarise. Make the full destination more valuable.

### 3. Create an AI-answer baseline

Record a relevant question set, the answers shown and the sources cited. Separate discovery from branded accuracy checks. Our [AI visibility guide](/blog/verify-improve-ai-search-visibility) explains the process.

### 4. Preserve effective SEO work

Keep maintaining the pages and technical conditions that already serve customers. Add evidence about AI discovery before making a wholesale budget shift.

For prioritising overlapping work, see [GEO vs SEO](/blog/geo-vs-seo).

### 5. Report business outcomes and uncertainty together

A useful report might say: "Search visits to these explanatory pages declined, AI citation observations increased on this fixed panel, and qualified enquiries were unchanged." That is more informative than declaring either total collapse or total success from one metric.

## What would stronger evidence of replacement look like?

It would require comparable longitudinal behaviour, clear definitions of a search or task, separation of website visits from active search activity, and enough detail to assess differences by audience and intent.

It would also need to distinguish adoption from substitution. A person starting to use an AI assistant does not necessarily stop using search engines.

For staffing claims, the evidence would need to measure completed work, quality, oversight and costs. Demonstrating that a tool can generate a draft is not the same as demonstrating that it can own a search programme.

## The practical answer

Plan for a search environment where answers, citations, websites and agents coexist. Invest in information and experiences that remain useful throughout that journey.

[GEO](/generative-engine-optimization) gives teams a way to examine generated-answer visibility. It should expand the evidence used to make decisions, not become an excuse to abandon search fundamentals or publish generic content at higher volume.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is SEO dead in 2026?

That claim is too broad. Some traffic patterns and tasks are changing substantially. Assess your audience, page types and outcomes before treating the whole discipline as obsolete.

### Should I stop writing informational content?

No blanket rule follows from lower click-through in some studies. Prioritise information with a clear audience, defensible evidence and a useful destination. Reconsider interchangeable summaries that add little beyond the answer already available elsewhere.

### Will ranking well guarantee an AI citation?

No. A result position and a citation in a generated answer are different observations. Track both where they matter instead of assuming a one-to-one relationship.
