## What is the best AI visibility platform?

**The best [AI visibility](/platform/ai-visibility) platform is the one that measures the engines, questions and markets you need, preserves evidence behind its scores, and fits your team's workflow.** Shortlist by use case: lightweight monitoring, category research, technical diagnostics and enterprise content operations are different buying decisions.

Consider [OtterlyAI](/compare/otterly-ai) for a small monitoring pilot and [Peec AI](/compare/peec-ai) for prompt-led marketing analysis. Ahrefs Brand Radar and Semrush suit broader research. [Scrunch](/compare/scrunch-ai) fits technical agent-experience work, and [Profound](/compare/profound) fits enterprise analysis and marketing workflows. CiteLadder is relevant when you want answer and source evidence alongside website and search context.

These editorial shortlists rest on documented capabilities; they are not test scores. Vendors describe the same category as AI visibility platforms, AEO tools (answer engine optimization) or GEO tools ([generative engine optimization](/generative-engine-optimization)); this comparison treats those labels as one market.

**Disclosure:** This comparison is published by CiteLadder, which sells a product in this category. We reviewed official product, pricing and help pages on 6 October 2026 and re-checked the Peec AI, Semrush and Profound pricing pages on 8 November 2026. We did not run paid trials or independently benchmark collection accuracy. Reconfirm plan details and prices before purchase.

The plan page also documents brand and competitor analysis, domain and URL source views, fan-outs, referral and crawler insights, and plan-dependent reporting and integration features.[\[2\]](#source-2)

**Check before buying:** the exact model mix, country limits and final price. ## Ahrefs Brand Radar: broad discovery and custom questions

Ahrefs distinguishes a pre-collected AI Visibility Index from Custom Prompts. The former helps investigate existing category coverage; the latter tracks questions you define. Its page also documents cited pages, fan-outs and adjacent web and bot analytics.[\[3\]](#source-3) _Reviewed 6 Oct 2026._

**Why consider it:** it suits research that begins with "Where does this topic appear?" as well as an existing Ahrefs workflow.

**Check before buying:** database coverage for your niche, collection cadence, custom-check consumption and the relevant package. A prompt derived from search-query data is not automatically a measured human conversation in an AI app. The pricing page returned localised currency, so read the price from the actual checkout configuration.

## Original pricing insight: compare the workload, not just the prompt count

Our public-page review found incompatible purchasing units: prompts, model choices, checks, domains, users, platform credits and external provider usage. Do not collapse them into a single "cheapest tool" table.

For example, OtterlyAI's 15-prompt, four-engine, daily configuration implies **1,800 scheduled prompt-engine observations over a 30-day month**. Peec's 50-prompt, three-model daily Starter configuration implies **4,500**.[\[1\]](#source-1)[\[2\]](#source-2)

Calculation: prompts × selected engines × scheduled days. These are theoretical scheduling counts, not verified successful answers, equal-quality samples or a claim that one package is better. Retries, country variants, engine availability and collection methods can change the practical workload.

| --------------- | ------: | ------: | ---: | ---------------------: | ---------------------------------- |
| OtterlyAI Lite  |      15 |       4 |   30 |                  1,800 | $29/mo, reviewed 6 Oct 2026        |
| Peec AI Starter |      50 |       3 |   30 |                  4,500 | $95/mo monthly, checked 8 Nov 2026 |

Method: prompt and engine counts are taken from each vendor's pricing page and multiplied by 30 daily runs. Peec's page states the 4,500 figure itself. Date of calculation: 8 November 2026 (Peec) and 6 October 2026 (OtterlyAI). We did not include Semrush because its prompt-tracking engines differ from its database engines, so the same formula would not be comparable.

Ask each vendor to price the same brief:

> Track 50 specified unbranded questions across three named surfaces in one country, daily for 30 days. Preserve raw answers and source URLs, allow three users, and provide an export. State all extras, collection methods and retention limits.

That makes proposals more comparable than asking only for "50 prompts."

## What should you verify in an AEO tool demo?

Verify ten things: engine coverage, collection method, prompt controls, evidence, competitor handling, history, technical diagnostics, referrals, actions and portability. Use this list in every demo.

1. **Engine coverage:** name the actual surface, not merely the model family.
2. **Collection method:** distinguish consumer UI, API and pre-collected index data.
3. **Prompt controls:** test your own questions, intents, locations and languages.
4. **Evidence:** open a raw answer and trace its citations.
5. **Competitors:** review brand matching and ambiguous-name handling.
6. **History:** check collection start dates, retention and changes to old records.
7. **Technical diagnostics:** distinguish robots-policy checks from observed crawler requests.
8. **Referrals:** distinguish AI visits and conversions from bot activity.
9. **Actions:** ask how recommendations are derived and what a person must verify.
10. **Portability:** export a sample and recalculate one reported metric.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are AEO tools and AI visibility platforms the same thing?

In practice, yes. AEO (answer engine optimization) tools, GEO (generative engine optimization) tools and AI visibility platforms all track how AI assistants answer a set of prompts and which sources they cite. The labels reflect vendor positioning more than product differences. Compare collection method, evidence and plan units, since the category name tells you little.

### Is there an open-source AEO tool?

Yes. CiteLadder publishes its tracker as an open-source, self-hosted build alongside the hosted version, and other open-source trackers exist. Self-hosting shifts engine and API costs to your own accounts and keeps prompts and answers on your infrastructure; confirm the licence terms and the engines covered before relying on one.

### How much do AI visibility tools cost?

Among the entry plans confirmed in this review, prices run from $29 to $99 per month. OtterlyAI Lite was $29 (reviewed 6 Oct 2026), Peec AI Starter is $95 monthly and Semrush AI Visibility Base is $99 per domain billed annually (both checked 8 Nov 2026). Profound lists a free 7-day trial and custom Enterprise pricing. Engine add-ons, users and domains are often extra.

### Do I need a dedicated AI visibility tool or can my SEO suite do it?

An SEO suite is enough when you want AI reporting beside existing search work and its engines and prompt limits fit your questions. A dedicated tool suits teams that need raw answers, source records or engines the suite does not cover. Test both against the same question set before you decide.

### How many prompts do I need to track?

Start with the questions that map to a business decision, then size the plan to that list. Entry plans in this review cap prompts at 15, 25 or 50, so a focused pilot fits. Multiply prompts by engines by days to compare workloads across vendors, as shown in the pricing section above.

### How often should I review AI visibility data?

Review on a steady schedule that matches how often you can act, such as weekly for a pilot. Several tools collect daily, but answers vary between runs, so read trends across several collections. Keep the raw answers so you can check any change that surprises you.
