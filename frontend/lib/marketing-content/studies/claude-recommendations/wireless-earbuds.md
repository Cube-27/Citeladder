CiteLadder asked Claude Sonnet 5.5 20 buyer questions about wireless earbuds, three times each, in three conditions: from memory, with web search available, and told to search first. Sony was the brand Claude named most often in every condition. When Claude was told to search, the rest of the top five shifted: Samsung and Jabra dropped out, while Soundcore and CMF came in. This page reports what Claude said through the Anthropic API. It is not a ranking of which earbuds are best.

## What we asked Claude

The 20 prompts cover the questions buyers type when they shop for earbuds. Some ask for an overall pick. Some describe a buyer or a use case. Others ask about budget, a single feature, or a comparison shortlist.

Examples:

- "What are the best wireless earbuds you can buy right now?"
- "Best earbuds for sleeping on my side comfortably?"
- "Good wireless earbuds under $50?"

Each prompt ran three times in each condition, for 60 answers per condition. A separate model listed the brands each answer named, and code did the counting. The requests did not include a current date.

## Brands Claude named from memory

With no tools, Claude named 37 distinct brands across 60 answers, about 6.2 per answer. Sony appeared in 77% of answers. It was named first 20 times and appeared in all three runs for 15 of the 20 prompts.

Bose (67%) and Apple (65%) came next. Each was named in every run for 10 prompts. Samsung (53%) and Jabra (45%) completed the top five.

Soundcore appeared in 38% of answers, but it was named first 9 times. That is more often than Bose or Apple. It tended to lead on budget questions.

Run agreement from memory was 63.9%. A brand named in one run often did not return in the next run of the same prompt.

## How often Claude searched when it could

When web search was available but not required, Claude searched in 15 of 60 answers. It ran 19 searches in total. For most earbud questions it answered from what it already knew.

The results in this condition stayed close to memory. Sony and Bose each appeared in 70% of answers, Apple in 65% and Samsung in 62%. EarFun entered the top ten at 30%.

## What changed when Claude was told to search

When the system prompt told Claude to search before answering, it searched in all 60 answers and ran 254 searches. The answers named more distinct brands (51) but fewer per answer (5.1). Run agreement fell to 52.5%.

Sony stayed the leader. It appeared in 60% of answers and was named first 28 times, with an average position of 1.4. Apple (43%), Soundcore (40%) and Bose (37%) were also in the top five. Sony, Bose and Apple were in the top five in both conditions.

Two brands left the top five:

- Samsung fell from 53.3% from memory to 15% when told to search.
- Jabra fell from 45% to 13.3%.

Two brands entered it:

- Soundcore moved from 38.3% to 40%.
- CMF reached 25%. It was not in the memory top ten.

Technics and JLab also appeared in 20% of searched answers. Neither was in the memory top ten.

## What Claude searched for

Most queries read like a shopper's search. 82.8% of them contained the word "best". Examples:

- "best true wireless earbuds 2025 rtings wirecutter"
- "best budget earbuds under $50 review Soundcore EarFun Anker"
- "Sony WF-1000XM6 vs AirPods Pro 3 vs Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds Gen 2 comparison"

44.5% of queries included a year. 2025 appeared in 106 queries and 2026 in 8. The requests gave no date, so Claude chose these years on its own. Some queries named review sites or brands directly, so what Claude already knew shaped what it looked up.

None of the answers carried inline citations.

## Sites Claude's searches returned

A small group of review sites showed up again and again. soundguys.com appeared in the results for 53 of the 60 searched answers (88%). tomsguide.com appeared for 52 (87%), rtings.com for 44 (73%) and techradar.com for 42 (70%).

whathifi.com, scarbir.com, recordingnow.com, techgearlab.com and rollingstone.com followed. treblab.com, an earbud brand's own site, appeared for 22 answers (37%).

## What an earbud brand can take from this

From memory, Claude leaned on a familiar set of brands. When it searched, the answers followed the review sites it found. Brands with strong showings on review lists held their place or gained. Samsung and Jabra were named much less often when Claude searched.

A few practical points:

- Coverage on the review sites listed above matters for searched answers. These sites appeared in the results for most of the 60 answers.
- Claude searched for specific needs like running, budget or reliability. A product that a reviewer names for one of these needs has a clear route into the answer.
- Queries carried years, mostly 2025. A current review that names the model year can match those searches.
- Answers varied from run to run. One test tells you little. CiteLadder repeats each prompt to measure how consistently a brand appears.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which earbud brand did Claude name most often?

Sony, in every condition. From memory it appeared in 77% of answers. When told to search it appeared in 60% and was named first 28 times. This shows what Claude said, not which earbuds are best.

### Does Claude search the web for earbud questions on its own?

Rarely. With search available, it searched in 15 of 60 answers and ran 19 searches. When told to search first, it searched in all 60 answers.

### Which sites did Claude's searches return most?

soundguys.com, tomsguide.com, rtings.com and techradar.com. soundguys.com appeared in the results for 53 of the 60 searched answers.

### Will the Claude app give the same answers?

Not necessarily. These results come from Claude Sonnet 5.5 through the Anthropic API at low effort, with no current date given. The Claude apps can behave differently, and answers also varied between runs of the same prompt.
