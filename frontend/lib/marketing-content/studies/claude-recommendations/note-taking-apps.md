CiteLadder asked Claude Sonnet 5.5 20 buyer questions about note-taking apps. Each question ran three times in three conditions: from memory, with web search available, and with an instruction to search first. Obsidian was named in 77% of answers from memory and stayed the most-named brand when Claude searched. This page reports what Claude said through the Anthropic API. It is not a ranking of which app is best, and the Claude apps can answer differently.

## What was asked

The 20 prompts covered five kinds of buyer intent: general picks, specific buyers or use cases, budget or free options, feature-led needs, and comparison shortlists. Each prompt was phrased the way a person might type it.

Examples:

- "What's the best note-taking app right now?"
- "Which note app works best with an iPad and Apple Pencil?"
- "Note-taking app with end-to-end encryption for private notes"

Other prompts asked about college lecture notes, small teams sharing meeting notes, developers storing code snippets, open source options, and offline apps with local markdown files. The requests gave no current date. Another model listed the brands in each answer, and code did the counting.

## Which brands Claude named from memory

With no tools, Claude gave 60 answers and named 91 distinct brands, about 7.4 per answer. Two brands appeared far more often than the rest.

- **Obsidian** was named in 77% of answers and named first 20 times.
- **Notion** was named in 70% of answers and named first 14 times.
- **Microsoft** was named in 55%, **Apple** in 48%, and **Google** and **Joplin** in 40% each.
- **Logseq** and **Standard Notes** reached 23%, **Evernote** 22% and **Bear** 20%.

Microsoft, Apple and Google appear here as companies, as the brand listing recorded them.

Consistency across the three runs was moderate. Run agreement was 58.6%. Obsidian was named in every run for 14 of the 20 prompts, and Notion for 13. Apple, despite appearing in nearly half of answers, was named in every run for only 5 prompts. The top three brands took 27.2% of all mentions, so the rest of the answers spread across a long list.

## When search was only available

When web search was available but optional, Claude did not use it. Across 60 answers it ran 0 searches. These answers were memory answers in practice.

The results looked much like the no-tools condition. Obsidian was named in 78% of answers and Notion in 73%. Microsoft, Google and Apple followed. Answers named slightly more brands, about 8.7 each, and run agreement was 63.8%. For this category, a brand's visibility in Claude through the API depends on what the model already holds unless something prompts it to search.

## When Claude was told to search

With a system prompt telling it to search first, Claude searched in all 60 answers and ran 280 searches. The answers got shorter on brands. It named 76 distinct brands, about 5.8 per answer. Run agreement dropped to 51.2%, and the top three brands took a larger share of mentions at 30.3%.

- **The leader held.** Obsidian stayed first, named in 65% of answers and named first 14 times.
- **Kept in the top five:** Obsidian, Notion, Microsoft and Google.
- **Left the top five:** Apple, which fell from 48% from memory to 33%.
- **Entered the top five:** Joplin, which rose from 40% to 47%.

Notion was named in 58% of answers and named first only 6 times, down from 14 from memory. Microsoft was named in 53% of answers and named first 14 times, level with Obsidian. Apple Notes also appeared as its own entry in 13% of answers. Evernote and Logseq each fell to 15%. Only 1 answer included inline citations.

## What Claude searched for

Most queries were short list-style searches. 62.7% contained the word "best", and 44.9% contained a year. Of the queries with a year, 118 used 2025 and 11 used 2026. Since the requests gave no current date, the year came from Claude's own assumption.

Sample queries, copied as Claude wrote them:

- "best note-taking apps 2025 comparison"
- "Notion vs Obsidian vs Evernote vs OneNote vs Apple Notes pros cons"
- "best snippet manager for developers 2025 massCode Obsidian Notion Raycast"
- "Obsidian vs Logseq vs Joplin vs Notable local markdown offline"

Several queries already named brands. Claude often searched for apps it had in mind and then checked them against what the results said.

## Which sites the searches returned

One site stood out. **atlasworkspace.ai** was returned in 42 of the 60 searched answers, or 70%. After that came **zapier.com**, **techradar.com** and **en.wikipedia.org**, each in 45% of searched answers, and **clickup.com** in 43%.

Next were **thedigitalprojectmanager.com** and **saner.ai** at 40% each, **toolfinder.com** at 37%, **alternativeto.net** at 35% and **scribe.com** at 30%. The mix includes editorial review sites, software vendors that publish comparison pages, a directory and an encyclopedia. These are the pages Claude read before writing its shortlists. ## What a note-taking brand can take from this

- **Memory matters most by default.** With search optional, Claude never searched. What it already associates with your brand decides whether you appear.
- **Search changes the shortlist.** When told to search, answers named fewer brands and the top five shifted. Apple dropped out and Joplin moved in.
- **Comparison pages feed the answers.** The sites returned most often were roundups and comparison pages. Appearing accurately on pages like those on atlasworkspace.ai, zapier.com and techradar.com puts your brand in front of Claude when it searches.
- **Use-case pages count.** Claude searched for specific needs such as developer snippets and offline markdown. Clear pages for each use case give it something to find.
- **Check dated content.** Claude added a year to almost half its queries, mostly 2025. Roundups with a current year in the title may match those searches.
- **Measure repeatedly.** Run agreement ranged from 51.2% to 63.8%. One check is not enough to know where you stand.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which note-taking app did Claude name most often?

Obsidian. Claude named it in 77% of answers from memory and 65% of answers when told to search. This reflects what Claude said, not a judgment of which app is best.

### Did Claude search the web when it was allowed to?

No. With search available but optional, Claude ran 0 searches across 60 answers. It searched only when the system prompt told it to, and then it searched in all 60 answers.

### Which brands changed the most when Claude searched?

Apple fell out of the top five, from 48% of answers from memory to 33% when told to search. Joplin entered the top five, rising from 40% to 47%. Notion stayed in the top five but was named first far less often.

### Do these results match the Claude app?

Not necessarily. The study used Claude Sonnet 5.5 through the Anthropic API at low effort, with no current date in the requests. The Claude apps can use different settings and can give different answers.
