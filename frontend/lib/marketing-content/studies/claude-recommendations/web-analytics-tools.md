CiteLadder asked Claude Sonnet 5.5 20 buyer questions about web analytics tools, three times each, in three conditions: from memory, with web search available, and told to search first. From memory, Plausible was the brand Claude named most often. When told to search, Matomo took that place. This page reports what Claude said through the Anthropic API. It is not a ranking of which product is best, and the Claude apps can answer differently.

## What was asked

The 20 prompts cover the ways a buyer might ask about web analytics. Some ask for a general pick. Others name a use case, a budget, a feature or a shortlist. Examples:

- "What's the best web analytics tool for tracking website traffic?"
- "Best privacy-friendly analytics tool that doesn't need a cookie banner?"
- "Give me a shortlist of 5 web analytics tools with pros and cons"

Other prompts asked about a small Shopify store, a SaaS startup tracking product usage, GDPR compliance, self-hosting and heatmaps. Each prompt ran three times per condition, giving 60 answers per condition. The requests gave no current date. Another model listed the brands in each answer, and code did the counting.

## Brands Claude named from memory

With no tools, Claude named 67 distinct brands across 60 answers, an average of 7.8 per answer. Plausible appeared in 72% of answers and was named first 16 times. Matomo followed at 68%, then PostHog at 55%, Fathom at 53% and Mixpanel and Amplitude at 42% each.

The list leans toward privacy-focused and product analytics tools. Google Analytics appeared in 33% of answers, but when it appeared it tended to come first. It was named first 15 times, with an average position of 1.6.

Answers were fairly consistent across runs. Run agreement was 69%. Plausible was named in every run for 13 of the 20 prompts, and Matomo for 12.

## How often Claude searched when it could

When web search was available but not required, Claude rarely used it. It searched in 3 of 60 answers, with 14 searches in total. The brand mix stayed close to the from-memory results. Matomo appeared in 73% of answers and Plausible in 70%, with Plausible still named first most often, 18 times. Simple Analytics showed up at 40%. In this category, having search available changed little, because Claude mostly answered from what it already knew.

## What changed when Claude was told to search

When the system prompt told Claude to search first, it searched in all 60 answers and ran 282 searches. Answers got shorter on brands. It named 63 distinct brands, an average of 6.4 per answer. Run agreement dropped to 58.1%.

- **Leader:** Matomo, at 68% of answers. Plausible, the leader from memory, fell to 58%.
- **Kept in the top five:** Plausible, Matomo, PostHog and Fathom.
- **Left the top five:** Mixpanel, from 42% from memory to 30%.
- **Entered the top five:** Umami, at 35%. Its own share was 38% from memory, so it moved up because others fell further. It was named first 6 times, with an average position of 2.7.

Google Analytics appeared in 23% of answers but was still named first 11 times. The brand list also shows "Google" as a separate entry at 25%, which reflects how answers referred to it. None of the 60 answers included inline citations.

## What Claude searched for

The queries read like a buyer's research notes. They mix comparisons, pricing checks and accuracy questions. Examples:

- "best web analytics tools 2025 comparison pros and cons"
- "Google Analytics 4 vs Plausible vs Matomo accuracy ad blockers data loss"
- "Plausible Analytics pricing self-hosted nonprofit discount"

40% of queries contained the word "best". 40% included a year. Of those, 113 used 2025 and 2 used 2026. The requests gave no current date, so the year came from Claude itself. Several queries named specific brands, often putting Google Analytics 4 next to privacy-focused tools.

## Which sites the searches returned

The sites that came back most often were mostly vendor blogs and comparison pages. posthog.com was returned in searches for 50% of answers. uxcam.com followed at 42%, then seline.com and bootstrap.build at 40% each, and amplitude.com at 37%.

The rest of the top ten were analytics-alternatives.com, swetrix.com, openpanel.dev, humblytics.com and plausible.io. Several of these are smaller analytics vendors that publish comparison content. Their pages reached Claude's searches even when their own products were not among the brands Claude named most.

## What a web analytics brand can take from this

From memory, Claude already has a clear picture of this category, and it leans toward privacy-focused tools. If your brand is not in that picture, a user who does not trigger search may never hear of it. When search was optional, Claude searched in only 3 of 60 answers.

When Claude does search, the results can shift. Mixpanel dropped out of the top five and Matomo took the lead. The sources that came back were largely comparison and alternatives pages published by vendors. A few practical points:

- Publish clear comparison pages that answer the questions buyers ask, such as pricing, GDPR, self-hosting and ad blocker accuracy.
- Keep pricing and feature pages current. Claude's queries checked pricing and free tiers directly.
- Watch for dated content. Most dated queries used 2025, so pages with a clear year may match more searches.
- Track both conditions. Your share from memory and your share after search can differ.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which web analytics brand did Claude name most often?

From memory, Plausible, in 72% of answers. When told to search, Matomo, in 68% of answers. This shows what Claude said, not which product is best.

### Did Claude search the web on its own for these questions?

Rarely. With search available but not required, Claude searched in 3 of 60 answers. When the system prompt told it to search, it searched in all 60 answers.

### Why does Google Analytics rank lower in share but often come first?

Claude named Google Analytics in fewer answers than several privacy-focused tools, but when it did, it usually listed it first. From memory it was named first 15 times, with an average position of 1.6.

### Do these results match what I see in the Claude app?

Not necessarily. These results come from Claude Sonnet 5.5 through the Anthropic API at low effort, with no current date given. The Claude apps can use different settings and give different answers.
