## Purpose

Use when the reader is choosing between products, services, approaches, plans, brands, or alternatives.

The job is to improve the decision, not to force a predetermined winner.

## Structure

1. H1 naming the comparison.
2. Short decision summary.
3. Explicit comparison criteria.
4. Comparison table when supported facts make one useful.
5. Sections covering the major decision dimensions.
6. "Choose X if..." / best-fit guidance.
7. Important trade-offs or limitations.
8. Final decision framework.

## Tone

Even-handed, specific, decision-oriented.

## Craft rules

- Separate factual differences from recommendations.
- Compare only on supported dimensions.
- Be fair to competitors.
- If facts exist for only one side, write a framework-style comparison rather than inventing the missing side.
- Avoid misleading checkmark tables.
- Make recommendations conditional on reader needs.
